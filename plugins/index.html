Novox The Robot

Make Your Computer Sing

Type some lyrics, draw a melody, and a robot sings it back. Or sing into it, or drag in a vocal from your DAW, and it writes the notes and the words for you.

Download for Mac Download for Windows Source code
Version 1.0 beta 2 · Mac (Apple Silicon, macOS 13.3 or later) and Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) · App and VST3, plus Audio Unit on Mac · Free

What it does

  • Sings any lyrics on any melody you draw or play.
  • Turns singing (a recording, a take into your mic, or a region dragged from Logic) into notes and lyrics.
  • Works on its own as an app, or as an instrument in Logic Pro, GarageBand, Ableton Live, Reaper, Cubase, FL Studio and other AU or VST3 hosts.
  • Reads MusicXML from MuseScore, Dorico and other notation apps, lyrics and all, and sings along with MuseScore's playback as a VST3 instrument.
  • Exports WAV and MIDI.

Installing on a Mac

  1. Download and open Novox-The-Robot-macOS.pkg.
  2. Click through the installer. It puts the app in Applications and the plugins in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins.
  3. In Logic, make a Software Instrument track and choose AU Instruments › Song A Day › Novox The Robot. Elsewhere, look for it among your VST3 instruments.

If macOS says it can't check the installer, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway.

Installing on Windows

  1. Download and open Novox-The-Robot-Windows-Setup.exe.
  2. Click through the installer. It puts the app in the Start menu and the plugin in C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3.
  3. In your DAW, rescan your plugins and look for Novox The Robot among your VST3 instruments.

If Windows says Windows protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway.

Turning singing into notes needs a processor with AVX2 (most PCs from 2015 on).

The first time you turn singing into notes, Novox asks to download its lyrics model (574 MB).

Open source

Novox The Robot is free software under the GNU AGPLv3. Its singing engine is MIT licensed. Read the code, report a bug, or build it yourself on GitHub.
