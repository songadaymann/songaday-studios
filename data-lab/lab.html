      What held up

      Each result compares songs with others from the same month, week or year. Two reviewers re-ran each one.

      Weekend songs are mellower.

      On Saturdays and Sundays about one song in three is excited, silly, angry or otherwise high-energy. On weekdays it's closer to one in two.

      On the road, songs run longer and get fewer views.

      1,329 of the 6,538 songs were written away from home.

      +17 slonger than at home (95% range +12 to +22 s)
      −28%views, against the same month at home
      −31%anxious songs: 33 where 48 were expected

      News songs come within a day and get twice the views.

      169 songs closely match a headline from the same day or the two before. Chance would give 48.

      Around US elections and inaugurations, 35% of songs are about politics. The rest of the time it's 9.7%.

      Clean-shaven songs get more views.

      About 1.7 times the views of bearded songs from the same month, or 1.44 times with topic, mood and the rest held equal. Clean-shaven days also have fewer tired songs: 65 where 108 were expected.

      The songs got angrier around 2016.

      Dark moods went from about 11% of songs to about 21% in 2016 and 2017 and stayed there. Almost all of the rise is angry songs, mostly about politics. Sad songs show no trend.

      What gets watched.

      Songs about Apple get 4.7 times the views of other songs from the same month. Politics, Trump, TV and pop culture also beat the average. Poetic songs, commissions and conference songs get about half.

      Silly, excited, angry and happy songs get 1.4 to 1.6 times the views of pensive ones. Baby Yoda alone has a quarter of all views, and the top 1% of songs have three quarters.

      Superstitions

      None of these made a difference.

      Full moons

      High-energy moods on 42.8% of 222 full-moon songs, 41.6% on other days. Tempo, length and minor keys are the same.

      Mercury retrograde

      Anxious, confused, angry or annoyed moods on 11.6% of 1,263 songs, 10.5% otherwise.

      Friday the 13th

      Unhappy moods on 15.2% of 33 songs, 17.1% on other days.

      Supermoons and eclipses

      Supermoons: 45.7% high-energy against 41.6%. Eclipse days: 11.1% unhappy against 17.2%.

      Planet parades

      When the planets bunch up in the sky (tightest: April 20, 2024), songs are no wilder and no faster.

      Star signs

      Neither the Sun's sign nor the Moon's shifts the moods once the season is accounted for.

      Monday blues

      Tired or sad songs on 13.9% of Mondays, 14.3% of other days.

      Rainy days

      Sad songs on 6.5% of wet days, 7.0% of dry ones in the same month.

      Hot and cold

      Warm days aren't faster, cold days aren't more minor, and sunshine doesn't lift the mood.

      Found along the way

      • Weather

        #1397 “Hurricane Song One” went up the day before Hurricane Sandy hit. The next day, #1398, had the strongest wind of any song day: 38 mph with 1.3 inches of rain.

      • Weather

        Songs with “snow” in the title fall on snow days 44% of the time. All songs: 7%. #3683 “It's Sunny But It's Cold Outside” was clear with a high of 17 °F.

      • Extremes

        Hottest song day: #6422 in Palm Springs, 115 °F. Coldest: #2246 in Jersey City, −8 °F. Wettest: #286 in Berkeley, 3.2 inches of rain.

      • Sky

        #4373 “Jupiter and Saturn” was written the day of their closest meeting since 1623, and #5577 on the day of the 2024 total solar eclipse.

      • News

        Written the same day as the story: Bob Dylan's Nobel, the first close-up of Pluto, the end of the Opportunity rover and the Ever Given.

      • Views

        #3974 “Baby Yoda Baby Baby Yoda” has 9.8 million views. The median song has about 400. #1618 “SOHCAHTOA” gained 283,000 views between August and October 2026, when school went back.

      • Lengths

        Shortest: #5097 “This Is The Whole Song”, 5 seconds. Longest: #4908, 19 minutes 37 seconds. The median is 2:13.

      • The sheet

        The public spreadsheet stops at #5101 (December 19, 2022). A find-and-replace that fixed instrument typos also rewrote seven YouTube IDs: “bAsE45R51bs” became “BassE45R51bs”.

      • Keys

        79% of songs with a known key are major. C leads (784 songs), then F, D and G. Most of 2010 (#358 to #727) has no minor keys, because of how keys were written down that year.

      Over the years

      Plain counts. These weren't tested like the results above.

      The main instrument changes every few years.

      Baritone ukulele from 2011 to 2014, acoustic guitar from 2014 to 2018, synths in 2020 and 2021.

      Length and tempo barely move. The median song is between 1:51 and 2:34 every year, and the median tempo between 95 and 108 bpm until 2026, which is at 120.

        How this was done

        Songs come from the Song A Day spreadsheet and catalog, checked against YouTube. Weather for each song's place and day is from Open-Meteo. Moon phase, planet positions, retrogrades and eclipses were computed for 5 pm New York time. Headlines are from Wikipedia's Current events portal, 100,638 of them. Views are from YouTube in October 2026.

        67 questions were tested by shuffling songs within the same month, week or year, then corrected for the number of questions. Two reviewers re-ran every result that looked real with different methods. They kept the ones above and downgraded four: moods don't change between the cities he lived in, seasons don't shift the moods, the politics link is mostly election weeks, and “darker over time” was a jump in angry songs.

        The data (one row per song, 121 columns), the code, every result and both reviews are at github.com/songadaymann/songaday-data.
